Government Property

Chapter 1

It was too late to save Eamon Ellis.

The bastard had run away whilst his father had been plotting him an escape plan. Now, less than an hour before processing, he let himself be found.

Eamon was a sausage. Rope wound around his entire body, keeping his arms by his sides and his legs tightly bound together. Three of his father’s men held him afloat by gripping the loops. Except the one at the front.

He was gripping Eamon’s throat.

As the door to his father’s quarters opened, a floral scent rushed in, replacing the air in his nostrils with whatever his father had sprayed. The men carried him deeper until they reached another set of doors. One of them knocked softly. “He has been recovered,” he said through the door.

They immediately slid open, morning light filtering through the windows and into the office. It was the kind of light that could only be felt at high altitudes. He realised he was in one of his father’s insufferable penthouses. At the top of a mountain.

The men dumped Eamon, his face planting onto the ground.

“You are such a bastard,” Resthanov’s voice came, his shadow towering over Eamon.

“You’re a little late,” Eamon muttered, his hair hanging over his eyes, obscuring the rest of his face.

“You think this is funny, don’t you?” His father parted his hair with his shoe and then tipped his chin up.

Their eyes met for a brief second. His father had traded his usual accessories for a plain suit and forehead creases.

Even his clothes knew it was an empty victory.

Resthanov’s gaze narrowed. He stepped back, only to boot Eamon in the ribs so hard his body rolled onto its back.

“Ow—”

“I told you not to run.”

Eamon lay there, his back aching. The men had already handled him roughly enough. He didn’t want a round two from his father.

“Why’d you run?” his father asked. Eamon responded with aggressive ceiling staring.

“Pick him up,” he ordered the men.

“Here we fucking go,” Eamon said under his breath as they hauled him up.

“I asked you. A question.”

Their black eyes stared each other down.

“What?” Eamon finally responded.

“Why’d you run?” Resthanov sighed, rubbing his forehead. He looked more stressed than angry.

“Because,” Eamon said flatly. “I’m due for service, and if you hide me, you’ll get us all killed.”

“Killed?” He laughed. “I? Is this what all this is about? Because I can assure you—”

“You and everyone else who holds our name.” Sometimes he wondered if his father should be checked for delusions.

Resthanov shook his head. The risks? He’d been taking them all his life. As soon as he’d gotten the selection notice, he’d made the preparations.

Fake identities, transfer routes, and a great plastic surgeon. “You know what you’re walking into. Your life is ruined.” Resthanov took a breath, pulling a coin from his pocket and twiddling it between his fingers. It was too late to do anything now. “Sit him down.” He tossed his men a pair of scissors, finally affording Eamon some dignity.

One of the men pushed him down whilst another slid a wooden seat under him.

Seconds later, the restraints around Eamon came off. He rubbed his raw wrists and stood back up to stretch. The worker on the right twitched, bracing for whatever Eamon might do next.

He sat back down, one leg over the other, staring at his old man. His father leant into the silence, letting out a slow breath.

Eamon huffed. “You’re being dramatic. Sure, it’s mandatory, and for life. But I’m being offered work. I’ll clock out like any normal person, and because of our background, I’ll probably end up in management.” It was a good deal, if you asked him.

“How is that any better than working RIGHT HERE?!” his father barked. “FOR ME, WITH FAMILY?!”

It wasn’t. But Eamon knew better than to admit that; his father might come up with an even more hysterical last-minute plan.

“I’m not really a fan of mixing family with business anyway,” Eamon replied. The last project he’d worked on with his cousin had actually lost the company a couple of million. The goalposts had shifted somewhere along the way from making money to stabbing each other in the back.

“I want to go, alright?” He’d be paid just fine and keep his idiot dad from getting their entire family killed. Win-win.

The office door swung open, slamming against the wall. His mother marched in wearing black heels and a simple maroon blazer, her face all scrunched up. Eamon was sure she was going to smack him with the purse.

“Idiot!” She smacked him across the head with her red handbag. “Running? Where on earth did you get that idea from?”

It wasn’t a particularly new or original one. Teenagers had been doing it for years. “Ow—” He winced. “I’m sorry!” He was not.

But then, seeing that he was okay, her shoulders eased and she hugged him tight, burying his head in her neck. “We just wanted to help. You didn’t even let us do that.” His mum held him tighter.

He didn’t dare pull away.

“You could’ve just said that you wanted to go. You didn’t have to run.”

But the three of them knew that if Eamon had let those words come out of his mouth, he would’ve been locked away until further notice.

“I know.” An idea struck Resthanov. “Just claim illness.”

“Do I look ill?” Eamon scoffed. He did cardio and weightlifting five days a week, with a personal trainer who corrected even the slightest tilt in his posture.

“He means mental illness,” his mum said. “Not a bad idea.” The couple held each other’s gaze in mutual approval.

A knock came at the door.

Then came the definitive slide open.

A man in a simple white shirt and black suit walked in, holding a tablet. Several men trailed behind him like they were infesting the room.

“Eamon Ellis? It’s time to be processed.”

Resthanov’s heart lurched.
