C.U.L.T.

Chapter 1

Marcus stood in the foyer, his eyes travelling over the marble-slabbed walls before settling on the monument at the centre of the room. It was several storeys high, water pouring from its eyes and running over its curves into the pool below. The statue was a hairy thing, every strand of fur carved into the stone. It stood broad, huge jagged wings spanning out across the foyer.

“What is that?” Velon looked up, jaw half on the floor.

“Several storeys of a crying hairy winged bastard,” Marcus muttered under his breath.

Worshippers walked past them in neutral-toned cloaks, their glances lingering. Marcus looked down at his black attire. “Why didn’t you tell me they had a dress code?”

“Marcus!” a man called out from across the foyer. He walked towards them in a pure white suit, his hair dyed to match. Beside him was a woman in the same pale attire. She had bright, pale skin, natural white hair and eyes that held both red and blue.

People bowed as the couple cut through the crowd.

“You sure this is a good idea?” Velon said. “They have a bit of a… reputation… you know…”

Marcus waved off his concerns. “Don’t make me laugh.”

“We meet at last.” The man held out his hand first, a snake cuff wrapped around his wrist.

Marcus slowly took his hand, his fingers grasping firmly. “Indeed, Your Honour.” His eyes flicked over to the man’s wife. She stood there quietly, looking at him, and yet, not quite. Marcus turned to her. Part of him couldn't believe he was about to do this. He dropped to his knees and bowed, forehead touching her feet. “Your Radiance—”

She stumbled, stepping back. Anon gripped her arm, steadying her. Marcus paused, lifting his head off the ground. “No?” He sat up. “I was told it was custom.” At least that's what his associates had told him. You bowed to the Whiteborns here, especially if it was the leader's wife.

“The hand, not the feet.” Anon chuckled.

Marcus got back up. “Right.” He took her hand and pressed his forehead to it.

“Not to worry. I'm sure any first-timer would make that mistake. Anyway, follow me.” It was time they got down to business.

Marcus walked quietly beside Anon as they left the foyer and headed deeper into the building. His eyes lingered on the Whiteborn again. It wasn't often a man brought his holy albino wife to a business meeting about drugs.

“Just this way.” Anon led them through what could only be described as a maze of passages. Eventually, he opened the door to his study.

Study was one word for it.

The door opened onto a vast, stadium-like room. Stairs descended to a lower level, where a long white table ran down the centre, surrounded by white sofas. A television was mounted on the opposite wall.

Anon took his wife's hand and led her down the stairs. The men followed quietly.

Marcus sat opposite her. Her gaze remained lowered, her world shrinking to the fingers in front of her. “Tea? Coffee?” Anon asked the men. They both shook their heads. “Or beer, perhaps?”

“No, we're good, really—” Velon began.

“Water then,” Anon said, turning to his wife, “darling, would you go get our guests some water?”

Marcus glanced at her. Anon had only just sat her down.

Regardless, Asley got up and crossed to a fridge tucked into the far corner. She pulled a tray from the top.

“Right. The reason I called you here.” Anon rotated the wedding ring on his finger. About time, Marcus thought. “I’d like to think we have a good business relationship, you and I.” Anon smiled, showing his teeth.

Marcus studied him. The soft features, the dyed white hair. He looked far too young to be running a place like this.

“You’ve been supplying us for years,” Anon continued. “In fact, you’re one of our biggest suppliers.”

“And you’re one of our biggest consumers.” It was true. They’d been doing business for years. So when the particularly large order came through, he knew he had to go see the man himself. He just hadn’t expected Anon to be twenty-five.

Anon clasped his hands in front of him. “I want you to be my sole supplier,” he said bluntly.

Marcus had figured that much. “That can be arranged. I’ll personally drop off every shipment myself.” And receive the cash in person too.

Asley placed a bottle of water in front of him, her eyes tentative. She wore a light perfume, a floral scent he couldn’t quite place. “But I’m presuming this wasn’t the only reason you wanted to see me,” Marcus said.

Anon laughed, loud and unrestrained, his eyes brightening. “I see. You’re smart too!”

“…Yes.” Marcus stared at him.

“Okay, I’ll get to the point.” Anon pulled out a key and reached under the table, fumbling with something out of everyone’s view.

“Let me.” Asley finally spoke when she noticed him struggling with the lock again. Her fingers were much nimbler than his.

“I've been meaning to change this for a while,” Anon told Marcus as his wife pried at the drawer. “I've just been too busy managing everything.”

When the thing finally rattled open, he pulled out a hardback ledger. It was plain white with gold edging, reflecting the establishment.

Marcus didn't look at the thing. He looked at the man, waiting for an explanation.

“You know,” Anon said. “We don't say no to anyone.”

Marcus was aware of that. “Right.”

“And we have quite a few… rougher people under our care.” Anon slid the ledger closer. “They need work. This could be the start of something important for both of us.”

Marcus looked at the ledger. “Two in one, huh?”

“That depends on you.” Anon grinned at them. “We'd love it if you joined our Lineages and Trusts.”

Marcus' eyebrows rose. He felt Velon go unnaturally still beside him.

Then Marcus broke into slight amusement. “It's a compelling offer. You have a very loyal… bunch… but I'm afraid we're far too rough for your taste.”

His organisation had a penchant for drawing blood, terror and starvation. The cult took in the homeless and fed them. It was like making a milkshake curry and asking why it didn't taste nice.

Anon’s lips quirked in amusement. “And doesn’t the Lord demand sacrifice for His provisions?” As far as he was concerned, they were two sides of the same coin.

An alarm cut through the conversation.

Anon lifted his head towards the wall clock. “Well, look at that. It’s time for lunch. Would you care to stay?”

As far as Marcus knew, lunch would be accompanied by pre-meal rituals. This meeting had run much longer than he’d intended anyway. He got up. “Next time. When I’m here with your…package.” He gave the couple a soft nod. “I’ll consider your offer.”

Another man, supposedly an employee, appeared from the entrance. Apparently, he’d been standing there the whole time. “I will now escort you out.”

“I would love to,” Anon told the men, “but I have to open the meal ceremony.”

“Not at all,” Marcus replied, picking up the ledger. He walked back up the stairs, feeling Anon's eyes on his back and the weight of the silence.

After the doors shut, Anon looked at his wife. “Well, that went well, didn’t it?”

Asley nodded. “I guess, so far.” She didn’t think they’d agree to his proposal, though.

Anon got up. “Come on, you must be hungry.”

Marcus and Velon were dropped back to the foyer. It was now filled with many more people heading towards the congregation. He walked back to the statue, staring up at it. Quietly, he took out a stack of cash from his pocket and placed it on the ledge.

“Why are you doing that?” Velon asked.

“Because business is good.” Marcus smiled slightly, and then he walked off.
